The internet goes out sometimes. The power follows. The road above Calca closes when the rain is heavy enough. And the community still has to eat, teach, heal, and keep its tools running.

That is the problem we are working on. Not as a thought experiment, but as a daily one.

## What Ayni means

*Ayni* is a Quechua word for sacred reciprocity. You help me with my roof; I help you with your harvest. Not because we traded, but because the community works when the work moves in both directions.

We did not invent this idea. It is thousands of years old and still alive in the valley where we live. We are taking it seriously as a way to build things: every part of the system exists because it serves the others.

## What we are trying to do

Most communities are not designed. They happen. People share some institutions and call it a community. That works until it does not.

We are trying to build something where food, energy, health, learning, and creativity are not separate departments, but parts of one system. The land feeds the kitchen. The kitchen feeds the people who tend the land. The people who teach also build. The tools they build keep everything running.

We have not figured this out. We are asking the question: what does it take for a place to be genuinely self-reliant? Not isolated, not independent. Able to stand on its own feet while staying in relationship with the wider world.

## How we think about technology

The right tools make self-reliance possible at a scale hand tools alone cannot reach. So we care about technology. But the question we ask is simple: *does this help the community stay on its feet?*

If a tool requires a server we do not own, or a connection we cannot count on, or a company that might change its mind, we look for another way. The technology we build is designed to work without the internet, to keep data where the people who made it can reach it, and to run on hardware a community can maintain itself.

We are not anti-internet. We use it. But we do not build on the assumption it will always be there.

## Where we are

This is happening in Calca, in the Sacred Valley of Peru. The valley sets the constraints. The seasons set the rhythm. We follow both.

Seven wings orbit this idea: sanctuary, wellness, energy, art, learning, games, and technology. Each one answers part of the question. None of them is the point by itself.

These notes are where we write about the work, the place, and the ideas behind it. Nothing polished, nothing sold. Just what is happening and what we are thinking about while it happens.
