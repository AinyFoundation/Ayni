**iOS Safari layout collapse, and the two viewport traps behind it (2026-08-07).** Reported as "everything gets messed up when scrolling, things move upwards then sideways out of focus, sections unreadable". A screenshot from the device showed the whole page rendered at roughly 60% with the SECOND panel's frame visible at x~750 on a 390px viewport — which is the tell: Safari had widened the document to the strip's full `200vw` (780px) and zoomed out to fit. Headless WebKit never reproduced it (scrollWidth == clientWidth at every scroll offset), because the trigger is the real device's dynamic toolbar, which Playwright cannot emulate.

Two fixes, both root-cause:

1. **`html { overflow-x: clip }`.** The 200vw strip lives inside an `overflow: hidden` sticky wrapper, which is supposed to be the end of it — but on iOS that containment gave way mid-scroll and the document widened. `clip`, NOT `hidden`, is essential: `overflow: hidden` on the root makes it a scroll container and breaks `position: sticky`, and this page is built almost entirely from sticky pins. The wrapper moved to `overflow: clip` for the same reason. Verified after: 0 horizontal overflow across the entire page in WebKit, and the wrapper, the offerings pin and the header all still stick.

2. **`vh` -> `dvh` on every pinned surface** (hero runway + wrapper, offerings container + pin + its grid rows, WelcomePanel's image caps), each keeping a plain `vh` line first as the fallback. On iOS `vh` is the LARGE viewport — the height the page would have with the toolbar hidden — so a 100vh pin is taller than the screen while the scroll-driven progress is measured against the REAL scrollport. The two disagree by the toolbar's height, and by a CHANGING amount while it collapses mid-scroll. That mismatch is what shifted content upward as you scrolled.

Also in this pass, from the same report: the hero social marks were redrawn larger (34px, up from a cramped 30px) with stroke settings hoisted onto the `<svg>` so every glyph carries identical weight — the Facebook "f" had been drawn as a detached tick that read as a stray mark. **Twitter/X removed** at the user's request; Instagram and Facebook remain. The row now clears the bottom chrome via `bottom: calc(var(--spacing-s-5) + env(safe-area-inset-bottom, 0px))`, which with the dvh change puts the toolbar outside the panel entirely.

**RULE: never size a pinned/scroll-driven surface in `vh`. Use `dvh` with a `vh` fallback, or the pin and its own progress will disagree on iOS.**

**Reduce Motion pinned the strip to its END state (2026-08-07) — the real cause of "the first section never loads on mobile".** `animations.css` blanket-applies `animation-duration: 0s !important` under `(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)`. For a TIME-driven animation that means "do not play". For a SCROLL-driven one it means the animation is instantly at its end state and stays there — so the strip sat at `translateX(-100vw)` permanently, the hero was off-screen before anyone scrolled, and the second panel was all that ever showed. Reproduced at scrollY 0 in BOTH Chromium and WebKit with Reduce Motion on (heroX -361px); invisible in every earlier test because none of them enabled it, and iOS users switch it on far more often than desktop users do.

`animations.css`'s own header anticipated this ("the hero's scroll-driven animations re-assert `animation-duration: auto` in their own components — a zero duration would snap a scroll-linked animation to its end state") but NO component actually implemented it any more; the new `@supports (animation-timeline: view())` block was the first real consumer and inherited the trap. The exemption now lives next to each scroll-driven animation: `.panels`, `.panel-hero` (+page.svelte) and the noscript navbar clip (app.html). `!important` is needed only because the blanket rule uses it.

The exemption is correct, not a workaround: the slide is not decoration, it is how the second panel is reached, and nothing moves that the reader did not move by scrolling. Verified under Reduce Motion in both engines — hero at p=0, mid-slide at p=0.5, welcome at p=1, navbar clip tracking, reload still landing on the hero.

**RULE: any future scroll-driven animation must re-assert `animation-duration: auto !important` under reduced motion, or it will silently pin itself to its end state.**

Instagram and Facebook marks sit at the bottom right; the geometry they share with the hero corner was lifted out of `+page.svelte` into **`src/lib/social.ts`** (`SOCIAL_MARKS`, keyed against `Messages['chrome']['social']` exactly as `$lib/nav` is keyed against `chrome.nav`) and their labels moved `home.hero` → `chrome.social`, because the same two links now render on two surfaces. **Their hrefs are still `'#'`**, held once as `SOCIAL_URLS` in `src/lib/config.ts` — fill those in and both surfaces light up. Two findings worth carrying: (1) **`--color-clay` cannot carry text.** Paper on it is 3.11:1 and ink on it 4.43:1 — the mid-tone fails WCAG AA in *both* directions at 14px. New primitive `--color-clay-d` (`color-mix`, clay 70% + ink 30%, 4.82:1 against paper) is the token for any clay *ground*; `--color-clay` stays an accent. Paper may not be dimmed on it either — even 10% toward the ground is 4.24:1 — so footer hierarchy is size/weight/tracking only, never `opacity`. (2) **`logo-icon.svg` is a bad export**: measured from its rendered alpha, on its 16:9 canvas the ink is 60.5%×47.5% and centred at (58.5%, 74%) — low and right. At `height: 48px` (what the old footer used) the visible mark rendered 23px tall and off-centre, which is why it never looked placed. Nothing consumes it now; anywhere it is used again, oversize the box until the *ink* is the wanted size and `transform: translate(-8.5%, -24%)`, or re-export the asset properly. `.rainbow-line` is not deleted, it is promoted: the seven hues now run along the crest on a paper keyline (three wings are under 2.7:1 against the ground and vanished without it). Verified headless in Chromium at 1440/820/390 on `/` and `/blog`, `npm run check` 0 errors, `npm run i18n:check` clean.

**Also 2026-08-08.** `ReviewPopup` mobile header used `padding-top: env(safe-area-inset-top, var(--spacing-s-3))` — the fallback only applies where `env()` is *unsupported*, not where the inset is zero, so on every notchless phone the title jammed against the top edge.
